Hyaluronic Acid 101 – What It Is and Why It Matters

Hyaluronic acid (HA) is a molecule naturally found in the skin, eyes and joints. Its main job is to bind water and keep tissues cushioned and hydrated. In skincare, we use topical HA to pull water into the upper layers of the skin and improve surface hydration.

HA itself is not an exfoliant or anti-ageing active like retinoids or acids. Think of it as a supporting player that makes your skin more comfortable and resilient so other steps can do their job better.

What hyaluronic acid can realistically do

  • Improve surface hydration and flexibility.
  • Make skin feel less tight or “crinkly” after cleansing.
  • Help makeup sit more smoothly over dry areas.
  • Support long-term tolerance of stronger actives.

What hyaluronic acid cannot do alone

  • Replace a moisturiser or SPF.
  • Erase deep wrinkles or lift sagging.
  • Fix barrier damage without other supporting ingredients.
  • Work without enough water in the routine (no cream, no hydration).

How Hyaluronic Acid Works in the Skin

Hyaluronic acid is a humectant – it binds water like a sponge. Different “molecular weights” (sizes) of HA sit at different depths:

High molecular weight

Sits closer to the surface. Great for immediate plumping and smoothing, but can feel sticky if used alone or in high amounts.

Mid molecular weight

Balances surface plumpness with slightly deeper hydration. Often used in daily serums for most skin types.

Low molecular weight

Penetrates a bit deeper. Can improve longer-lasting comfort, but may be irritating for very reactive skin if overused.

Multi-molecular HA serums combine several sizes to cover both surface and slightly deeper layers. For product comparisons, see: Hylaronic.com – Independent product comparison .

How to Use Hyaluronic Acid in a Routine

HA is flexible – it can be used morning or night. The key is to pair it with enough water and something to seal that water in.

  1. Cleanse: use a gentle, non-stripping cleanser.
  2. Hydrate: while skin is still slightly damp, apply a hyaluronic serum.
  3. Seal: follow with a moisturiser to lock in the water.
  4. Protect (day): finish with broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher.

If a product feels very sticky, try using less, applying to damp (not wet) skin, or layering it underneath a richer moisturiser.

Common Mistakes with Hyaluronic Acid

Most “HA doesn’t work for me” stories come down to one of a few simple mistakes.

Often manageable

  • Using too much product so it feels sticky.
  • Layering multiple HA serums for no real benefit.
  • Applying on bone-dry skin without any mist or moisture.

Warning signs – adjust or stop

  • Tightness or dryness that gets worse over time, not better.
  • Stinging or redness whenever you apply HA products.
  • New breakouts clearly linked to a heavy, film-forming formula.

Hyaluronic Acid FAQ

Can oily or acne-prone skin use hyaluronic acid?

Yes. HA itself is not oily or comedogenic. Oily and acne-prone skin often benefits from light, water-based hydration, as long as the full formula is non-comedogenic and not heavily fragranced.

Is “purging” a thing with hyaluronic acid?

No. Purging is related to actives that speed up cell turnover (like retinoids and some acids). HA should not cause purging. If you see breakouts, it is more likely a reaction to the overall formula (fragrance, occlusives) rather than the HA itself.

Can I combine hyaluronic acid with niacinamide, vitamin C or peptides?

Generally yes. HA plays well with most other skincare ingredients and is often used as a hydrating base layer under niacinamide, vitamin C and peptide serums.
